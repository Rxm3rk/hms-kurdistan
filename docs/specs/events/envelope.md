# Domain events: the CloudEvents envelope and delivery rules

The rules every HMS domain event follows, from the journal row to the consumer: the CloudEvents 1.0 envelope, the type naming rule, keys and ordering, schemas and their evolution, delivery, idempotent consumers, dead-letter items and replay. Written before the code (BUILD-PROMPT §3.2 step 2) in WP-0.3 for `PLT-CORE-04` ("domain event catalogue; transactional outbox", Appendix A.1). Sources: §2.1 SI-03, SI-10, SI-20, SI-21, §2.2 rule 6, §5.3 "Event layers" 2, §6.3, §6.7, §7 item 5, §8.5, §12.1; ADR-004, ADR-008, ADR-009, ADR-014; R08 §4.3; R11 §14; BP §7.6; K §7.5.

Companion specifications: the journal that carries the events (`docs/specs/modules/platform-journal.md`) and the catalogue of event names (`docs/specs/events/catalogue.md`).

**Status on 2026-10-19:** specification only, revised after two automated review lenses (correctness; clinical safety). The pure-Kotlin rules of §12 are built in WP-0.3 now; the transform, Debezium, Kafka, Apicurio, the dead-letter stores and the monitors wait for the cell (DN-040; §13). [P] marks this build's own choices. Open items carry local labels (OQ-E1, ADR-E1, KA-E1 and so on, §17); the integrator numbers them.

## 1. Layers

| Layer | What | Guarantees | Where specified |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Clinical journal | One append-only row per change, in the state change's transaction | Never updated; both time axes; gap-free key sequence | `platform-journal.md` |
| 2. Domain events | One CloudEvent per journal row and published major, published by Debezium and the journal's transform to Kafka | CloudEvents 1.0; schemas in Apicurio; backward-compatible evolution; 12-month deprecation; keyed by patient; at-least-once; idempotent consumers; nothing lost silently | This document |
| 3. Integration messages | HL7 v2, FHIR and Kurdistan formats generated by the Hub from domain events | Per-patient ordering (A08 never overtakes A01); MSH-10 idempotency; acknowledgement state; replay | §12; Hub specifications (Phase 1) |

(§5.3 "Event layers"; BP §7.6; ADR-004.) The domain-event catalogue "is a published product surface" (§5.3; R11 §17 #3): these rules are a contract with every consumer, the Hub, the `rcm` satellite and analytics included.

## 2. One event per journal row

- Every journal row becomes exactly one CloudEvent for each major of its type that is being published, and no event exists without its journal row. In the 12-month window after a breaking change (§7) one row is published as both majors; the event of the older major has the same `id`, because (`source`, `type`, `id`) together identify it [P]. A module never publishes to Kafka directly (ADR-009 "journal-as-outbox"; R08 §4.3: "a message is published if and only if the transaction commits").
- Events are facts in the past tense. They are never commands to another module (§5.2: modules call each other only through `api`; extension "through events and effects", §2.2 rule 8).
- **One named exception: work requests [P] (D14).** `TaskRequested`, `TaskCompletionRequested` and `TaskCancellationRequested` record that a module requested a task, or the completion or cancellation of a task, for one of its own aggregates. Each is a past-tense fact, written as version 1 of a new `TaskRequest` aggregate (`platform-tasks.md` §5.3), and catalogued with owner `platform-tasks` and every module as producer. `platform-tasks` reads these rows from the journal in the same database through its consumer tail, with a watermark, not through Kafka (`platform-journal.md` §4.8, §8.3; `platform-tasks.md` §4.4). A request older than the orphan grace without a task raises `TaskOrphaned` within the 5-minute bound (`platform-tasks.md` §4.3). The rows are also published like any other row. No other request type exists; a new one needs a catalogue entry naming its in-database consumer and the same orphan guarantee.
- The event carries the change's minimised data (`event_data`) as its payload, under the schema of its catalogued type (§6). The journal's full `payload` is never published (`platform-journal.md` ADR-J1).

## 3. Context attributes

CloudEvents 1.0 requires `id`, `source`, `specversion` and `type`; `datacontenttype`, `dataschema`, `subject` and `time` are optional. In HMS every one of them is set, so a consumer never has to guess [P].

| Attribute | CloudEvents | HMS value | From the journal row |
|---|---|---|---|
| `specversion` | required | `1.0` | constant |
| `id` | required | The journal entry's UUIDv7, lowercase hyphenated text. Unique across every source, so (`source`, `id`) is unique as the specification requires | `id` |
| `source` | required, URI-reference | `/hms/<module>`, the §5.2 module that wrote the row, for example `/hms/adt` [P] | `producer_module` |
| `type` | required | `hms.<CanonicalName>.v<major>` (§4), built by the mapper | `event_type`, `event_major` |
| `subject` | optional | `<AggregateType>/<aggregateId>`, for example `Encounter/0192…` | `aggregate_type`, `aggregate_id` |
| `time` | optional, Timestamp | **Recorded time**: when the system recorded the change, in UTC with `Z`, microsecond precision [P] | `recorded_at` |
| `datacontenttype` | optional | `application/json` | constant |
| `dataschema` | optional, URI | `urn:hms:schema:<CanonicalName>:v<major>`, the registry artefact of the type's major version (§7) [P] | `event_type`, `event_major` |

**Why `time` is the recorded time** [P] (integrator's shared architecture). The CloudEvents `time` is the time of the occurrence. For HMS the clinical occurrence time can be supplied by a user, back-entered from paper or back-dated (SI-10), so it is not a safe ordering or windowing field for consumers. The recorded time comes from the cell's own clock (ADR-026) and is what the system knew and when. The clinical time travels in the `validfrom` and `validto` extensions (§5), with its original offset. A consumer that shows or projects clinical time reads the extensions, never `time`. The AsyncAPI documents state this rule, and the Hub's conformance tests check that HL7 and FHIR clinical times come only from `validfrom` and `validto` (`ContractTest:hub-clinical-time-from-validfrom`, with the Hub; HAZ-0010).

## 4. The type naming rule

- **Format:** `hms.<CanonicalName>.v<major>`, for example `hms.EncounterAdmitted.v1`, `hms.TaskOrphaned.v1` [P].
- **`<CanonicalName>`** is the name in `catalogue.md`: PascalCase, past tense, unique across the whole catalogue. R11 §14 names are canonical; BP §7.6 names are aliases (`ChargeGenerated` for `ChargeCreated`, `EncounterMoved` for `PatientTransferred`) and **never appear in `type`** (§8.5; A.20; OQ-036). The catalogue code refuses an alias as a type (§12).
- **The owning module is not part of the type** [P]: ownership is catalogue metadata, so moving an event between modules does not break consumers. `source` tells which module produced a given event.
- **`<major>`** starts at 1 and changes only for a breaking change, which creates a new type published in parallel with the old one for at least 12 months (§7).
- **No sensitive category in a name** [P]: event names are generic (`ResultFinal`, never a name that reveals a diagnosis, a test or a sensitivity label), because the type travels in a header that infrastructure tools display and log (SI-21; CTRL-0021-5; §6).
- **The `hms.` prefix** is not a reverse-DNS name, which CloudEvents recommends for `type`. A domain the vendor owns has not been named (OQ-E1). The prefix is built by the mapper, never stored in the journal, so changing it touches no fact table; but it is a breaking change for every consumer, so it should be decided before the first event is published.

## 5. Extension attributes

CloudEvents extension names are lowercase ASCII letters and digits only and should not exceed 20 characters. HMS uses the extensions below, plus `traceparent` and `tracestate` from the CloudEvents Distributed Tracing extension. `EnvelopeRules.parse` refuses any other `ce_` attribute and ignores headers that are not CloudEvents attributes (for example headers that OpenTelemetry instrumentation adds) [P].

| Extension | Type | Required | HMS meaning | From the journal row | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `tenantid` | String (UUID) | always | The tenant | `tenant_id` | §6.4 |
| `facilityid` | String (UUID) | when the row has one | The facility | `facility_id` | §6.4 |
| `patientid` | String (UUID) | when the event concerns a patient | The patient's UUID, nothing else about the patient | `patient_id` | §5.3 C |
| `partitionkey` | String | always | The Kafka key: the patient id, the tenant id or the aggregate id, by the aggregate type's keying (D6; §9) (CloudEvents Partitioning extension) | `partition_key` | ADR-009; D6 |
| `keyseq` | Integer | always | The event's position among all events of its partition key: 1, 2, 3, … without gaps, in commit order | `key_seq` | §9 [P] |
| `validfrom` | Timestamp | always | Clinical (valid) time, with its **original offset** (RFC 3339 allows the offset) | `valid_from`, `valid_from_offset_s` | SI-10; §5.3; §8.1 P10 |
| `validto` | Timestamp | when the valid period is closed | End of the valid period, exclusive, with its original offset | `valid_to`, `valid_to_offset_s` | R08 §3.7 |
| `entrytiming` | String | always | The kernel `EntryTimingCode`: `CONTEMPORANEOUS`, `BACK_DATED`, `BACK_ENTRY`, `OFFLINE_CAPTURE`, `LATE_ARRIVAL`, `MIGRATED` or `EFFECTIVE_DATED` (D10). A code is labelled when `EntryTimingCode.labelled` is true; consumers show the label on screens, prints and exports, with display text keyed `entry-timing.<code>`. There is no separate label attribute | `entry_timing` | SI-10 "labelled"; CTRL-0010-4; CTRL-0010-5 |
| `changekind` | String | always | `CHANGE`, `AMENDMENT` or `ENTERED_IN_ERROR`. The Hub turns the last two into corrected or retraction messages downstream | `change_kind` | SI-06; §13.4 |
| `supersedes` | Integer | for `AMENDMENT` and `ENTERED_IN_ERROR` | The aggregate version this event amends or retracts | `supersedes_version` | SI-06 |
| `aggversion` | Integer | always | The aggregate's version after this change | `version` | §6.2 |
| `provenance` | String | always | Where the content came from: `user`, `device`, `ai-draft`, `migrated`, `interface` or `system`, so an AI draft stays labelled downstream (SI-07) | `source` | §8.1 P2, P8 [P] |
| `actorkind` | String | always | `USER`, `DEVICE`, `INTERFACE`, `MIGRATION_LOADER` or `SYSTEM` | `actor_kind` | D1 [P] |
| `correlationid` | String (UUID) | always | The command's correlation id | `correlation_id` | §6.2 |
| `causationid` | String (UUID) | when known | The event or command that caused this one | `causation_id` | [P] |

Every name is 20 characters or fewer. Enumerated values use the journal's own spelling, and `EnvelopeRules.parse` refuses an unknown value, so a consumer dead-letters an event it cannot read rather than defaulting it (for example to `CONTEMPORANEOUS` or `CHANGE`). The actor, on-behalf-of and device ids are staff and device identifiers; they travel in the event data's `meta.provenance` block (§6), not in attributes, so infrastructure tools never display them beside a patient key [P].

## 6. PHI rules: attributes, keys and payloads

The event backbone runs inside the cell (§5.1; ADR-007), and SI-20 keeps usable PHI there. Even inside the cell, headers and keys are seen by more tools than payloads are (broker tooling, connectors, traces), so they carry less [P].

**Attributes and keys.**
- The only patient datum in any attribute or key is the patient's UUID (`patientid`, `partitionkey`). Never a name, MRN, national identifier, date of birth, phone number, address, free text, reason, clinical code or display text (integrator's shared architecture; SI-20; SI-21).
- Other identifiers in attributes are opaque UUIDs (tenant, facility, aggregate, correlation). A UUIDv7 encodes its creation time to the millisecond; that is accepted for these identifiers [P].
- No sensitivity label in any attribute: a label beside a patient key would itself disclose the sensitive category (SI-21). Labels travel in the payload (below).
- **Logging and telemetry:** infrastructure and consumers may log only `id`, `type`, `source`, `aggversion`, `keyseq`, `entrytiming`, `changekind`, `provenance` and `actorkind`. `patientid`, `partitionkey` and `subject` are identifiers and are never logged, traced or put in metrics (§6.2 "identifiers are never logged"; CTRL-0020-7). The canary scan of WP-0.4 covers the event path.

**Payloads** [P] (each rule a design choice of this build, to be confirmed in the module specifications that define the schemas):
- A JSON object with camelCase property names, validated against the type's schema (§7). The journal stores it as `event_data`, separate from the full change data (`platform-journal.md` ADR-J1).
- **The `meta` block** is written by the journal appender, never by a module's mapper: `meta.provenance` (actor id, actor kind, on-behalf-of id, device id) and `meta.security`. **`meta.security` is required in every event whose type is patient-keyed, from v1: an array of label codes from the Trust Plane vocabulary, empty when the record has none.** A schema without it fails the contract test (§7). A missing property is therefore an error, never "no labels" (SI-21; CTRL-0021-5; OQ-E2).
- **Consumers that disclose data outside the clinical core** (the Hub, the front door, analytics, `rcm` exports) evaluate `meta.security` and purpose-of-use through the Trust Plane SDK's policy enforcement point before they disclose anything. They fail closed: an event with a missing `meta.security` or an unknown label code is dead-lettered, never passed on (SI-21 clauses a and d; CTRL-0021-3). This is a reading of SI-21 for the CSO and the privacy officer (OQ-E2), proved per disclosing consumer by `ContractTest:SI-21-disclosing-consumer-refuses-unlabelled-event` when each consumer is built.
- Times as RFC 3339 strings with their offsets; clinical and recorded times are separate properties wherever a payload carries both (§6.2).
- Quantities as `{ "value": "<decimal string>", "unit": "<UCUM code>" }`; never a bare number for a measured or dosed amount (SI-05; §6.2; §8.1 P6). Money as `{ "amount": "<decimal string>", "currency": "<ISO 4217 code>" }` (§6.2). Decimal strings, because many JSON parsers read numbers as binary floating point.
- Coded values as `{ system, code, display, originalText, version }` (§8.1 P5; §7 item 9). Payloads in tests and samples use only `urn:hms:synthetic:*` code systems, never real codes (§19.4).
- References to other aggregates as `{ "type": "<AggregateType>", "id": "<uuid>" }`.
- No document bodies, images or binaries: a reference to `platform-files` instead. No free text unless the catalogue entry for the type names the field and the reason in its `freeTextFields` (for example an override reason that consumers must report, SI-02); the contract test checks every `string` property without a pattern or enumeration against that list (§7; OQ-E3). A consumer that needs more reads the owning module's API through the policy enforcement point (§6.3).
- No field that ADR-008 designates for encryption appears in `event_data` (`platform-journal.md` ADR-J1).
- Size: bounded by the broker's message limit; a payload that needs more is a design error.

## 7. Schemas, registry and compatibility

- **Registry:** Apicurio Registry (§4.1; ADR-009). One artefact per event type and major version, artefact id = the CloudEvents `type` (for example `hms.EncounterAdmitted.v1`) [P]. Its versions are the compatible revisions of that major. `dataschema` names the artefact, not one revision (§3), because every revision of a major can read every event of that major.
- **Schema language:** JSON Schema [P]. The journal stores `event_data` as `jsonb`, the transform publishes it unchanged, the Hub and analytics read JSON, and the version catalogue already pins Apicurio's JSON Schema serde (`backend/gradle/libs.versions.toml`). Avro would add code generation and a second representation of every payload. This is a lasting contract decision (ADR-E1).
- **Open content model [P].** Every schema leaves `additionalProperties` unset or `true` at every level, so adding an optional property is compatible in both directions. The contract test refuses a schema that closes it.
- **What "backward-compatible evolution only" means here** [P]. §5.3 requires that evolution never breaks a consumer. With at-least-once delivery and replay, old consumers read new events (producers upgrade first) and new consumers read old events (replay from the journal). So each new revision must be compatible in both directions with every earlier revision of the same major: the registry rule is full transitive compatibility (Apicurio `FULL_TRANSITIVE`; the rule name to be confirmed against the pinned Apicurio 3.3 documentation). In practice:
  - allowed: adding an optional property; adding documentation;
  - breaking: removing, renaming or retyping a property; making an optional property required; narrowing a type or range; changing a unit, a code system or the meaning of a value; adding a value to a closed enumeration (unless the schema declares the enumeration open and consumers must handle unknown values);
  - consumers are tolerant readers: they ignore properties they do not know. `meta.security` is not optional for them: a disclosing consumer that cannot read it dead-letters the event (§6).
- **Breaking changes and the 12-month window** (§5.3; §6.3): a breaking change is a new major, a new type (`.v2`) with its own artefact. The producer publishes both majors in parallel for at least 12 months after the new major's first release (§2). One change still writes one journal row; how the old major is derived from it (a down-converter in the transform or a second mapping in the producer) is specified with the first breaking change [P]. The catalogue records the deprecation date and the earliest removal date. A deprecated property inside a major is never removed within that major; it is documented as deprecated and dropped only by a new major.
- **Contract tests** (§18.2 "consumer-driven … event contracts"; merge tier): every catalogued type with status `specified` or later has a schema file in the repository and sample payloads that validate; a schema change is checked for compatibility against all earlier revisions before merge; each consumer's contract test runs against the producer's samples. The AsyncAPI documents of §6.3 are generated from the catalogue and the schemas when the first types are published.

## 8. Transport: Kafka topics and keys

- **Binary content mode** of the CloudEvents Kafka protocol binding: the payload is the Kafka message value; every attribute is a header named `ce_<attribute>`; `datacontenttype` is the `content-type` header; the message key is `partitionkey` [P]. The journal's transform builds the headers (`platform-journal.md` §10.1).
- **Topics** [P]: per journal database, one **patient** topic (every event keyed by a patient) and one **platform** topic (events keyed by a tenant or an aggregate), routed by the journal's `event_stream` column. Topic names come from deployment configuration, never from code (§6.2).
- **Why one patient topic, not one per module** [P]. Kafka orders messages only within one partition of one topic. A patient's events come from many modules (an admission, then orders, then results), and the Hub must never send them out of order (§12.1 "A08 never overtakes A01"). Keying every patient event by patient in one topic keeps all of one patient's events from **one journal database** in one partition, in commit order (ADR-009; R08 §4.3). The `rcm` satellite has its own journal database and its own topic pair, so its events are not ordered with the core's; a consumer that needs both must not assume an order between them.
- **Producer settings** [P]: the connector's producer is idempotent and keeps order on retry (idempotence on, with in-flight requests bounded as the Kafka client requires for ordering). A setting that could reorder a key is a configuration error.
- **Partition counts are fixed** once a topic is in use [P]. Changing a topic's partition count changes which partition a key maps to, so one patient's events could be consumed out of order across the change. Growth means a new topic and a planned cut-over at a quiescent point, never an in-place change.
- **Retention:** the journal is the long-term source (§11), so topic retention only has to cover consumer outages. It is deployment configuration, at least 7 days, matching the queues that must survive WAN loss (§16.3 L1) [P]. Dead-letter items do not live on topics (§10).
- **Debezium** (Kafka Connect, run by the cell) reads the journal's write-ahead log in commit order (`platform-journal.md` §10). Only the journal's connector writes to the patient and platform topics, except a privileged gap re-publication (§11).
- **Transport independence [P].** ADR-009 keeps NATS JetStream as the fallback if C9 fails (§4.1). Every rule here except the Kafka header names maps to JetStream: one ordered stream per key space, the key as the subject token, the same attributes as message headers, consumer acknowledgement in place of offsets. The key sequence, ledgers, dead-letter stores and monitors do not depend on Kafka.

## 9. Delivery and idempotent consumers

- **At-least-once** (§5.3; ADR-009): a consumer may receive an event more than once (a connector restart, a consumer crash after its work but before its offset commit, a replay). Nothing is lost silently: the connector resumes from its replication slot; slots survive failover (`platform-journal.md` DN-J1); and the publication watermark check finds and re-publishes any row that never reached its topic (`platform-journal.md` §10.3; D15).
- **Idempotent consumers keyed on the CloudEvents `id` and the key sequence** (integrator's shared architecture; R08 §4.3) [P]. Each consumer keeps, in its own database, a processed-events ledger: the event ids it applied, and per partition key the last `keyseq` it saw. Processing one event:
  1. open a transaction in the consumer's database;
  2. look up the key's last sequence `n` in the ledger, in that transaction;
  3. if `keyseq ≤ n`, or the id is already recorded: skip, commit, then commit the offset;
  4. if `keyseq > n + 1`: a gap. Park the whole partition key, whatever the consumer's parking scope (§10), and raise the gap alarm; the missing event arrives by gap re-publication or replay;
  5. otherwise (`keyseq = n + 1`): apply the effect if the type concerns this consumer, record the id and set `n = keyseq`, all in the same transaction; commit; then commit the offset.
  A consumer reads every event on its topic and records the sequence even for types it ignores, so it sees every gap. The first event of a key that a consumer ever sees sets its baseline, unless the consumer was started from the beginning of the journal [P]. A crash between the commit and the offset commit causes a redelivery that step 3 skips. The ledger is insert-only for ids and kept for the life of the consumer, because a journal replay can redeliver any past event.
- **Effects of a consumer are themselves commands**: if a consumer changes its own aggregates, it does so through its own command handler. Each effect's idempotency key is derived, never the bare event id: `evt:<consumerId>:<eventId>:<effect>`, so two consumers of one event, or two effects of one event, never collide (`platform-journal.md` §4.6) [P].
- **Ordering:** within a partition key, events arrive in commit order, and `keyseq` proves it; across keys nothing is promised. `aggversion` shows order and gaps per aggregate; consumers that need per-aggregate completeness use it.
- **One aggregate, one key for life:** an aggregate's partition key never changes after it is created (`platform-journal.md` J-14), so its events never move between partitions.
- **Patient merges [P].** A merge is a link, not a move (SI-08). After `PatientMerged` (keyed by the survivor), the replaced patient's open aggregates keep their old key, while new aggregates use the survivor's. One person's events then span two keys. A consumer that needs per-person order (the Hub first) must resolve the merge link and order across both keys, for example by holding the replaced key's later events until it has applied the merge. How, and whether `empi` moves open aggregates to the survivor, is `empi`'s to specify in WP-1.2 (OQ-E4).

## 10. Dead-letter items: owned, alarmed, never lost

"No hidden dead-letter topics" (ADR-009). "Undeliverable messages land in owned queues that alarm after 5 minutes" (§5.3; BP §7.6). "An owner, visible acknowledgement state, an owned dead-letter queue that alarms at 5 minutes, and a replay console. Zero silent drops" (§12.1).

- **Every consumer has an owner** (a §5.2 module and its squad) and **its own dead-letter store** [P]: a table in the consumer's own database, holding each failed event unchanged (same id, same headers, same data) with its failure record. Items and parked events are never deleted, so no topic retention can remove them (§8). No consumer shares another's store, and none uses a broker-wide default.
- **The first failure is recorded durably, at once [P] (D15).** On the first failure of an event, before any retry, the consumer writes a failure record (event id, key, `firstFailureAt` from its injected clock, reason code) in a separate transaction. The 5-minute clock starts there. Retries never extend it.
- **When an event becomes a dead-letter item:** a schema-invalid payload, an unknown enumerated value or label (§5, §6), a business refusal the consumer cannot resolve, or a transient failure still failing after the consumer's retries. Retries are bounded so the move happens well inside the alarm limit.
- **Lifecycle** [P]: `OPEN` → `REPLAYED` (after a fix, §11) or `OPEN` → `RESOLVED_WITHOUT_REPLAY` (with a reason, never defaulted). Each item is an aggregate of type `DeadLetterItem` (owner `platform-journal`, written by the event library on behalf of the consumer module, as the `SYSTEM` actor from `hms.kernel.events`, a package on the kernel's `SYSTEM_PROCESSES` allow-list, kernel specification §7.2) whose changes are journal rows and events (`DeadLetterItemOpened`, `DeadLetterItemReplayed`, `DeadLetterItemResolved`; `catalogue.md` §4). Who resolved it, when and why is therefore audited, and "resolved without replay" is reportable on the safety dashboard. Permissions (WP-0.4): `events.dead-letter.replay`, `events.dead-letter.resolve`.
- **Parking keeps order** [P]: once an event of a key is dead-lettered or a gap is seen (§9), the consumer parks every later event in its parking scope (it records them in order in its own database) until the head is resolved, then processes them in order. **Each consumer's specification states its parking scope and why:** the partition key (the Hub, which must keep A08 behind A01) or the aggregate (for example the task engine, so an unrelated poison event does not hold back a patient's critical-result task). A consumer may skip-and-continue only if its specification states why order does not matter to it.
- **Parked events are visible and alarmed.** The dead-letter item's task shows how many events are parked behind it and the age of the oldest. A parked event of a type the consumer's specification marks safety-relevant is named in the task, and the task's escalation applies (CSO content). Parking is a cause in HAZ-J1 (`platform-journal.md` §17).
- **Every dead-letter item is owned work.** It raises a task through the `DeadLetterTaskSink` port (§12), which `platform-tasks` implements in `hms.platform.tasks.application.system` (`platform-tasks.md` §7.1). The item carries the owning team's queue code that the consumer's registration declares (`owningQueueCode`), which becomes the task's owner. When the item is replayed or resolved without replay, the library calls `DeadLetterTaskSink.resolved`, which closes the task. The task's SLA starts at the item's `firstFailureAt`, not when it was moved to the store, so the task and the alarm agree (`platform-tasks` §7.1 aligns). An unclaimed task in a routable queue breaches its SLA (`TaskSlaBreached`, with the operational alarm `DeadLetterItemAged`) and escalates; `TaskOrphaned` fires only if the task's holder becomes unroutable (`platform-tasks` §4.3). If the sink is unavailable, the consumer raises the operational alarm `DeadLetterTaskSinkUnavailable` and retries; the alarm of the next item does not depend on the sink.
- **Alarm at 5 minutes:** an item unresolved when `now ≥ firstFailureAt + alarmAfter` raises the dead-letter alarm (§6.7 "dead-letter queue items older than 5 minutes"). The task's SLA breach is the primary alarm; this library's evaluator over the failure records is the independent backstop when the sink is unavailable (D15; `platform-tasks.md` §7.1). The evaluator reads the failure records from the database, not the consumer's memory, and runs on continuous time, never paused by weekends or holidays (integrator's shared architecture).
- **A consumer that never writes a failure record is caught by the lag-age monitor [P] (D15).** A consumer in a crash loop (a deserialiser crash, out of memory) or one that is stopped or cut off from the broker creates no dead-letter item. An independent monitor outside the consumer's process alarms when any consumer group's oldest unconsumed event on any partition is older than the dead-letter limit, and raises an owned task for the consumer's owner. It waits for the cell (KA-E1); `ChaosTest:consumer-crash-loop-alarms-within-5-minutes` proves it there.
- **Configuration (D2):** the setting `platform.events.dead-letter-alarm-after` is owned and declared by `platform-tasks` (`platform-tasks.md` §8), registered in tenancy's `SettingCatalogue`: type `DURATION(PT30S, PT5M)`, vendor default `PT5M` (§5.3; §6.7), levels VENDOR_FOUNDATION, COUNTRY_PACK, TENANT and FACILITY, `SafetyInvariant` for SI-03 (CTRL-0003-1, CTRL-0003-5), `TightenOnly` with "shorter is stricter", `GOVERNED` (§2.1 preamble; K §5.1). A kernel library cannot import a module, so it declares nothing itself. `platform-tasks` resolves the setting and builds `DeadLetterAlarmPolicy` through its factory (§12; D11), and uses the same value for the dead-letter task's SLA span.

## 11. Replay

Four modes, all privileged, with a non-defaulted reason, and audited (gap re-publication may also be started by the publication watermark check as the `SYSTEM` actor, from `hms.kernel.journal` on the kernel's `SYSTEM_PROCESSES` allow-list) (§7 item 5 "replay"; §12.1 "replay console") [P]. Every mode that reads the journal (2 to 4) reads rows in `key_seq` order per partition key, so a patient's events are replayed in commit order, and publishes them unchanged, with the **same `id`** and `keyseq`.

1. **Offset replay** within topic retention: a consumer's offsets are moved back; the ledger skips what it already applied (§9).
2. **Journal redelivery** (`REDELIVER`) beyond retention: a replay job reads journal rows (by tenant, recorded-time range, event types, or patient) and publishes them to **that consumer's own replay topic**. The consumer reads its replay topic and the live topic through the same ledger: an event already applied is skipped, a gap parks the key (§9), so live and replayed events of one key cannot overtake each other. Other consumers see nothing.
3. **Rebuild state** (`REBUILD_STATE`), to rebuild a consumer's own state from scratch: as `REDELIVER`, but every event carries the replay marker in the consumer's processing context, and the consumer suppresses every external effect (HL7 or FHIR messages to other systems, notifications, task creation, charges) while it rebuilds. A consumer with external effects may reset its ledger only in this mode [P]. Old critical results therefore never reach clinicians again by replay.
4. **Gap re-publication** (`GAP_FILL`): the rows that the publication watermark check found missing go to the **shared** topic, started automatically by the check or by operations, in `key_seq` order (`platform-journal.md` §10.3). Consumers that parked the key at the gap resume; consumers that already have the event skip it by id and sequence.

A dead-letter item is replayed from its store together with the events parked behind it, in order (§10). Replay of events whose data carries sensitivity labels follows the same policy checks as the original delivery (SI-21).

## 12. What `hms.kernel.events` holds (built in WP-0.3)

Pure Kotlin, no CloudEvents SDK, Kafka, Debezium, Apicurio, Jackson or Spring types (the SDK pinned in the version catalogue is used only in infrastructure adapters, when the wiring is built). The package has no `domain` sub-package because it has no adapters in WP-0.3 (integrator's shared architecture). The kernel rule set applies to the whole package: framework-free, with `io.cloudevents`, `io.debezium` and `io.apicurio` in `FRAMEWORK_PACKAGES`, and no system clock (D7; `ArchTest:kernel-events-framework-free-and-clock-free`). ## 16. Acceptance criteria

1. Every event published from a journal row passes `EnvelopeRules.parse` with no violation; no alias ever appears as a `type`.
2. No attribute or key carries patient data other than the patient's UUID; no sensitivity label is in an attribute; logs show only the allowlisted attributes.
3. One patient's events are consumed in commit order, each applied once, under redelivery, restarts and replay; a lost event is detected as a gap (the §21 exit criterion, on Kafka once the cell exists).
4. A dead-letter item is owned, parks its scope, raises a task whose SLA starts at the first failure, and alarms 5 minutes after its first failure; the alarm age cannot be set above 5 minutes at any level; a stalled or crash-looping consumer alarms within 5 minutes.
5. A schema change that breaks compatibility in either direction fails the merge tier; a patient-event schema without `meta.security` fails it.
6. Replay delivers the same event ids and sequences, only to the requesting consumer (except gap re-publication), never re-fires external effects in rebuild mode, and is audited.

## 17. Open questions, proposals and known anomalies (local labels; the integrator numbers them)

- **OQ-E1: the `type` prefix.** CloudEvents recommends a reverse-DNS prefix for `type`. Which domain does the vendor own and want in every event type? Default `hms.` until answered; changing it after the first published event is a breaking change for every consumer, so it should be answered before the first `published` status.
- **ADR-E1: event payload schema language and compatibility mode.** Proposal: JSON Schema in Apicurio with an open content model, full transitive compatibility per major, tolerant readers, breaking changes only as a new major published in parallel for at least 12 months (§7). Context: §5.3 fixes Apicurio and "backward-compatible evolution only" but not the schema language or which direction "backward" protects; both are long-lived contract decisions with every consumer and the published catalogue (R11 §17 #3).
- **OQ-E2: sensitivity labels on events (a reading of SI-21 for the CSO and the privacy officer, §3.4 item 1).** SI-21 asks for labels and purpose-of-use to be enforced at the policy enforcement point for every channel, interfaces and analytics included. Kafka consumers sit behind the backbone, not behind the PEP. This spec's reading: labels travel only in the payload (`meta.security`, required in every patient event from v1, an empty array when none); infrastructure never sees a label beside a patient key; every consumer that discloses outside the clinical core evaluates labels through the Trust Plane SDK's PEP and fails closed on a missing property or an unknown label (§6). Also: should `BreakTheGlassAccess`, which reveals which patients' records were emergency-accessed, go to a restricted trust-plane stream that only `trust-audit` and the privacy office read, rather than the shared patient topic? Proposal: yes, a third stream, decided with this OQ.
- **OQ-E3: free text in payloads.** This spec forbids free text in payloads unless the catalogue entry names the field and why (`freeTextFields`, §6). Confirm, and list the event types that need free text (override reasons, cancellation reasons, configuration change reasons).
- **OQ-E4: per-person ordering across a merge (for `empi`, WP-1.2).** After `PatientMerged` one person's events span two keys (§9). Decide whether `empi` moves the replaced patient's open aggregates to the survivor through an audited workflow, or every per-person-ordered consumer holds the replaced key's later events until it has applied the merge. Test with merge-then-update ordering at the Hub.
- **OQ-E5: test names for the hazard log.** Propose adding `UnitTest:dead-letter-alarm-due-after-5-minutes`, `ChaosTest:dead-letter-alarm-within-5-minutes` and `ChaosTest:consumer-crash-loop-alarms-within-5-minutes` to CTRL-0003-5; `UnitTest:envelope-carries-no-phi-attributes` to CTRL-0020-7; and `ContractTest:SI-21-disclosing-consumer-refuses-unlabelled-event` to CTRL-0021-3 and CTRL-0021-5. Recorded here, not self-approved.
- **KA-E1: the delivery machinery does not exist until the cell.** No dead-letter store, parking, alarm wiring, lag-age monitor, replay job, Apicurio registration or schema file exists in WP-0.3; only the pure rules of §12 and their tests. Crash loops and stalls are therefore not alarmed yet. Hazard references: H5, H6 (HAZ-0003 CTRL-0003-5; HAZ-J1). Backlog: `PLT-CORE-04`. Closes with the WP-0.3 cell slice after WP-0.2 (DN-040). Shared with `platform-journal.md` KA-J2.
