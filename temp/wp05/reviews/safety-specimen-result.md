## Verdicts on the eight questions

1. **Specimen SI-01: the wristband scan is always required, there is no manual fallback, and labels print on demand at the bedside. Verdict: over-blocking. Not safe as the default.** The default stops outpatient phlebotomy. R1 is the polyclinic wedge, and §9.8's seam rule limits the LIS to the polyclinic's own outpatients. It also stops collection whenever a wristband is unreadable. It contradicts §9.7 "Every override of a failed scan is a reportable event", which assumes a reportable override exists. Forbidding labels printed in advance blocks the downtime label packs (§9.7, CTRL-0001-8) and the alias kits for mass-casualty and trauma patients (SAC-06). Paper-downtime collections cannot be back-entered at all. Babies and unconscious trauma aliases work only while a band is present and readable. The predicate's wording also does not fit a DataMatrix that carries the MRN and the encounter (§12). See safety-specimen-01, safety-specimen-02 and safety-specimen-03.
2. **Specimen SI-03: cancel, lost and entered-in-error paths, and a recollection task on every rejection and loss. Verdict: the paths and tasks are present, but three liveness holes remain.** There are four:
   - nothing creates the replacement specimen after a recollection;
   - nothing watches a tube that was dispatched and never received;
   - a specimen in process is stuck when one of its tests' results is cancelled while the order stays active;
   - `stored` has no lost path.

   See safety-specimen-05, safety-specimen-06, safety-specimen-07 and safety-specimen-10.
3. **Specimen SI-15: downtime accession blocks. Verdict: sound for clause c.** Numbers come from the site-prefixed block assigned to the device and are never reused. Internet loss alone is not offline (clause h). The server revalidates offline commands (clause f), and that is left to the command handler. The gap is the paper downtime path (safety-specimen-02), plus a stale order status during offline collection (safety-specimen-14).
4. **Result technologist validation on every release, preliminary included. Verdict: it does not block the urgent preliminary.** The technologist who would phone a critical potassium must check it anyway. Validating and releasing is that same human step, and filing never waits for the communication, so it adds no delay. The critical hand-off is never dropped: every alternative where `criticalValueFlagged` holds carries `handToCriticalResultService`. Autoverify excludes critical values, and NOT_EVALUATED counts as critical. Two defects remain:
   - **(a)** a value waiting for validation has no owner, no SLA and no priority, so a critical value can sit unvalidated (safety-result-03);
   - **(b)** "no policy for the test" counts as critical, which would flood the critical-result service (safety-result-02).

   The release guards also never check the specimen's state, which is the one critical finding (safety-result-01).
5. **Result autoverification: present, inactive, and gated by the Register plus the tenant flag. Verdict: SI-18 is mostly honoured and the path is correctly marked proposed.** The flag alone never suffices; status, stored opinion, expiry, version and jurisdiction are all checked; no Register entry exists, so the predicate is false for every tenant; Autoverify is only on the proposed registered → final edge. Missing: the opinion's **scope and intended purpose** (SI-18 clause b), and a tie between the rule-set version and the validated entry (safety-result-08). Approving the shared edge approves both commands on it (safety-result-13).
6. **Correction notices to the orderer and every acknowledger (H3); entered-in-error instead of delete (H6). Verdict: mostly sound.** Amend, Correct, RevisePreliminary, both Cancel transitions and entered-in-error all notify. Nothing is deleted. Gaps:
   - the final-after-preliminary notice leaves out the people who received a phoned critical result (safety-result-09);
   - corrections do not reach downstream copies, although entered-in-error does (safety-result-10);
   - open critical-result communications are not updated when the value is withdrawn or superseded (safety-result-04);
   - a result entered in error raises no task to find the right patient's result (safety-result-05).
7. **SI-17 routing for discharged patients; the "unknown encounter fails closed" reading. Verdict: failing closed is safe**, because it adds routing and never drops a result. Three problems:
   - it disagrees with the encounter machine's E-4 reading about final results not yet acknowledged (safety-result-06);
   - counting finished outpatient visits as discharges floods the queue (safety-result-07);
   - "no encounter" must be a recorded fact, not a missing link (safety-result-12).
8. **QC lockout guard on corrections: can it block correcting a dangerous wrong result? Verdict: no.** Entered-in-error has no QC guard. An amendment that withdraws a value adds no new measurement, so the QC check passes vacuously. Only new in-house measurements wait for valid QC, and §9.8 acceptance 2 requires that. Unchanged values are not checked again. The predicate does not say how a transcription correction is treated, and result.md does not describe the withdrawal path (safety-result-11).
